# 📄 CV and career toolkit

## Free CV builders
- 🆓 [Europass](https://europass.europa.eu): the standard European CV format, in many languages.
- 🆓 [Reactive Resume](https://rxresu.me): open-source, no paywall.
- 🆓 [Overleaf CV templates](https://www.overleaf.com/gallery/tagged/cv): LaTeX templates for clean, ATS-friendly CVs.
- 🎁 [Canva](https://www.canva.com/education/): designed CVs. Keep them simple; fancy layouts can break applicant tracking systems.
- 🎁 [FlowCV](https://flowcv.com): free tier available.

Ready-to-copy text templates are in [`/templates`](../templates).

## CV rules that work
- **One page** for students and recent grads (two for long experience).
- Put the most relevant items first: education, projects, experience, skills.
- Start bullets with **action verbs** and add numbers: *"Organised a coding workshop for 40 students."*
- Match keywords from the job or programme description.
- Save as **PDF** named `FirstName-LastName-CV.pdf`.
- Check country norms: photos and birth dates are common in some countries and discouraged in others.

## Motivation letter formula (1 page)
1. **Hook:** why this programme/job, in two sentences.
2. **Proof:** 2–3 experiences that show you are ready.
3. **Fit:** what you will gain from them and what you will contribute.
4. **Close:** your goal and a polite call to action.

## LinkedIn and portfolio
- Use a clear photo, a headline with your field and a short "About" section.
- Put projects on [GitHub](https://github.com) (code) or a simple portfolio page (design, writing).
- Connect with alumni of your target programmes and ask short, specific questions.

## Interview practice
- 🆓 [Pramp-style peer practice](https://www.pramp.com) and mock interviews with friends.
- Prepare **STAR stories** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for 5 common questions.
- Research the organisation and prepare 2 questions to ask them.

## Career centres
Your university's career service often offers free CV reviews and mock interviews. Use them.
