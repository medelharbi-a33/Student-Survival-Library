# 🛡️ Stay safe: avoiding scams

People searching for opportunities are a target. Learn the red flags.

## Red flags
- **You must pay** to apply for a scholarship, visa lottery, or "guaranteed" job.
- "Guaranteed admission" or "100% visa success".
- Pressure: "Pay today or lose your spot."
- Emails from free addresses (Gmail, Yahoo) pretending to be a university or embassy.
- Requests for payment by gift card, crypto, or wire transfer to a personal account.
- Offers you never applied for.
- Employers asking you to **buy equipment** with a cheque they send you.
- Vague contracts or none at all.

## How to verify
1. Find the organisation's **official website** yourself (do not use links from the message).
2. Check the email domain matches the official domain.
3. Search the organisation name with "scam" or "reviews".
4. Contact the university, embassy or programme directly.
5. For visas, use only **government websites** and official application centres.
6. For recruiters and agencies, check official registration and ask for written terms.

## Fees that can be legitimate
- University application fees (published on the official site).
- Language test fees and official visa fees.
- Insurance and translation costs.

These are paid to the official organisation, never to a random person.

## Protect your data
- Never share passwords, one-time codes or full passport scans with unverified people.
- Use strong, unique passwords and two-factor authentication.
- Be careful with job offers that ask for bank details before a signed contract.

## If you get scammed
- Stop all contact and save evidence (messages, receipts).
- Contact your bank immediately.
- Report to local police, your embassy and the platform where you found the scam.
- Warn others by opening an issue in this repo (without personal data).

## Copyright and piracy
Pirated books and course material can carry malware and legal risk. Use libraries, open-access sources and student discounts instead.
