# 🧭 Roadmap: from idea to studying abroad

A realistic timeline. Start **12 to 18 months** before your intended start date if you can.

## 12–18 months before
- [ ] Decide your field and 3 target countries (consider tuition, language, cost of living, visa rules).
- [ ] Check what your target programmes require: degree, GPA, language test, references.
- [ ] Start a language test plan (IELTS, TOEFL, TestDaF, DELF, etc.). See [language learning](06-language-learning.md).
- [ ] Make a spreadsheet: programme, deadline, requirements, fee, scholarship link, status.

## 9–12 months before
- [ ] Shortlist 5–8 programmes. Mix ambitious, realistic and safe options.
- [ ] Shortlist scholarships ([guide](01-scholarships.md)). Many deadlines fall in **October–February**.
- [ ] Ask 2–3 teachers or supervisors if they will write references. Give them your CV and a list of deadlines.
- [ ] Draft your CV ([toolkit](05-cv-and-careers.md)) and a master motivation letter.

## 6–9 months before
- [ ] Take your language test (leave time to retake).
- [ ] Get documents translated, certified or apostilled if needed.
- [ ] Submit applications. Submit early; portals crash on deadline day.
- [ ] Tailor each motivation letter. Never send the same one to everyone.

## 3–6 months before
- [ ] Accept an offer and apply for your visa ([guide](04-visas-and-study-abroad.md)).
- [ ] Prove funds if required (blocked account, bank statements, scholarship letter).
- [ ] Look for housing through the university first, then trusted platforms.

## 0–3 months before
- [ ] Book flights, buy insurance, arrange accommodation.
- [ ] Learn survival phrases in the local language.
- [ ] Join the university's international student group online.

## After you arrive
- [ ] Register your address / residence if the country requires it.
- [ ] Open a bank account and check student work-hour limits.
- [ ] Visit the career centre in week one.

> 💡 **Tip:** Applications are a numbers game plus quality. Many successful students applied to 10+ programmes or scholarships before getting a yes. Rejection is normal. Keep going.
