# 🛂 Visas and studying abroad

> ⚠️ Visa rules change often and depend on **your nationality**. This page points to official sources and explains the general process. It is not legal advice.

## The general process
1. **Get admission** (an offer letter) from a recognised institution.
2. **Check the visa type** for your nationality and course length.
3. **Gather documents.** Typical list:
   - Valid passport
   - Admission letter
   - Proof of funds (bank statements, blocked account, scholarship letter)
   - Health insurance
   - Language proof (IELTS, TOEFL, etc.)
   - Passport photos and the application form
   - Proof of accommodation (some countries)
4. **Book the appointment** at the embassy or visa centre. Slots fill fast.
5. **Apply and track.** Keep copies of everything.
6. **Prepare for the interview** if required: know your course, why this country and how you will fund yourself.

## Timelines
Apply for your visa **as soon as you are allowed**, often 3–6 months before the start date. Processing can take weeks to months.

## Money questions
- Tuition varies hugely. Many public universities in **Germany, Norway and others charge low or no tuition** (rules and exceptions apply; check each one).
- Budget for rent, insurance, transport, food and a safety buffer.
- Check **student work-hour limits** before counting on part-time income.

## Common mistakes
- Waiting for the final exam results before starting paperwork.
- Using unofficial "agents" who promise guaranteed visas. See [Stay safe](10-stay-safe.md).
- Submitting documents that are not translated or certified when required.
- Not reading the embassy page for your **specific** country of residence.

## After arrival
Register your address, get a residence permit if required, open a bank account and check what the university's international office offers.
