# 🇹🇳 Tunisia and North Africa

> 🚧 **Starter page.** It lists official institutions and general advice for students in Tunisia and North Africa. Rules, offices and links change, so **confirm everything on the official sites**. Tunisian students and alumni: please add what you know (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md)).

## Official institutions in Tunisia
- 🆓 [Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research](https://www.mes.tn): national rules, recognition of diplomas, public scholarship and mobility announcements.
- 🆓 [Orientation portal](https://www.orientation.tn): university orientation after the baccalaureate.
- 🆓 [Tunis Virtual University](https://uvt.rnu.tn): free online resources from Tunisian universities.

## Advising and cultural centres in Tunisia
These centres often run info sessions, language classes and test registration. Check their official pages for current services.
- **EducationUSA / AMIDEAST Tunisia:** advice on studying in the USA. See [amideast.org](https://www.amideast.org).
- **Campus France Tunisia:** the compulsory route for many applications to France. See [campusfrance.org](https://www.campusfrance.org) and look for the Tunisia page.
- **DAAD information in Tunis:** advice on studying in Germany. See [daad.de](https://www.daad.de/en/).
- **Goethe-Institut Tunis:** German courses and exams.
- **Institut français de Tunisie:** French courses, DELF/DALF exams and cultural events.
- **British Council Tunisia:** English courses and IELTS.
- **Erasmus+ National Office in Tunisia:** information on exchange and Erasmus+ calls. See [erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu).

## Language advantage
Many Tunisian students already speak **Arabic and French** and often some English. Add one more:
- **English:** opens the UK, Ireland, Canada, Netherlands, Nordic countries and most scholarships. See [language learning](06-language-learning.md).
- **German:** opens low-tuition universities in Germany and Austria. Start with A1 early because B1–C1 is often required.

## Practical points to check early
- **Diploma recognition and equivalence:** ask the target university what documents it needs and whether translation or legalisation is required.
- **Visa centres:** each embassy uses specific official centres. Check the embassy website for the right one and avoid unofficial "agents".
- **Money transfers and foreign currency:** ask your Tunisian bank about the **current rules** for paying fees abroad, student allowances and receiving money from outside the country.
- **Proof of funds:** requirements vary by country (blocked account, bank statements, scholarship letter).
- **Apostille or legalisation** of documents can take time. Start early.

## Local communities and experience
- Student clubs: IEEE, Enactus, AIESEC, JCI, university clubs.
- Hackathons and tech events in Tunisia and online: see [Opportunities](08-opportunities.md).
- Alumni networks: ask graduates who studied abroad about their timeline, costs and mistakes.

## Scams that target students in the region
- Facebook or WhatsApp "agents" promising visas, jobs in Europe or the Gulf, or guaranteed admission.
- Payments requested through personal accounts, cash or transfer apps.
- Fake scholarship emails asking for fees.

Read [Stay safe](10-stay-safe.md) before paying anyone.
